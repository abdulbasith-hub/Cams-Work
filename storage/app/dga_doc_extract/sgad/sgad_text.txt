Chapter4:StateGovernmentAuditDepartment
Establishment
In the year 1945, the Government appointed the Examiner of Local Fund Accounts as the Chief Auditor, State Trading Schemes, entrusting the office with the responsibility of conducting internal departmental audit of State-run trading schemes administered by the Government.(Paragraph 4.1.1)
EvolutionintoaSeparateDepartment
AstheresponsibilitiesoftheExaminerofLocalFundAccountsincreasedsubstantiallywiththeadditional function of Chief Auditor, State Trading Schemes, a separate department for the audit of State Trading SchemeswasformedunderG.O.Ms.No.449,Finance,dated03.05.1969,headedbytheChiefAuditor,State Trading Schemes.(Paragraph 4.2.1)
Subsequently, the Public Accounts Committee endorsed the recommendation of the Comptroller and AuditorGeneralofIndiaforestablishingastrongandindependentinternalauditsystemundertheFinance Department.Inpursuancethereof,theGovernmentrealignedthefunctionsoftheDepartmentofLocalFund Audit and the Chief Auditor, State Trading Schemes. As per G.O.Ms.No.598, Finance (Local Fund) Department, dated 03.08.1992, the Chief Auditor, State Trading Schemes was redesignated as the Chief Internal Auditor and Chief Auditor of Statutory Boards, and the department was renamed as the Internal Audit and Statutory Boards Audit Department as per G.O.Ms.No.848, Finance (LF) Department, dated 09.11.1995.(Paragraph 4.2.2)
Following an announcement made by the Deputy Chief Minister during the discussion on Demand for Grants for 2019–2020, the duties and responsibilities of the department were extended and realigned to cover Statutory and Non-Statutory Boards, Government Departments, and Government-aided Institutions. The department was consequently renamed as the State Government Audit Department as per G.O.Ms.No.264, Finance (LF) Department, dated 16.08.2019. (Paragraph 4.2.3)
The organizational structure of the audit system evolved over time through several key developments. Initially, the Local Fund Audit Department and the Chief Auditor for State Trading were established in 1969. Later, in 1995, the Director of Local Fund Audit became the Head of Department (HOD). Parallelly, the Internal Audit and Statutory Boards Audit Department was formed, headed by the Chief Internal Auditor and Chief Auditor of Statutory Boards, a role formalized in 1992 and designated as HOD in 1995. Eventually, these functions were consolidated under the State Government Audit Department in 2019, with the Director serving as the Head of Department.

Functionsofthe Department
TheStateGovernmentAuditDepartmentisresponsibleforconductingstatutoryandinternalauditofawide spectrum of institutions including Statutory Boards, Non-Statutory Boards, Government Departments, and Government-aided Institutions. Audit reports are issued within stipulated time frames, and paragraphs relating to serious irregularities are brought to the notice of the Government and the Public Undertakings Committee (PUC) of the Tamil Nadu Legislature.(Paragraph 4.3.1)

Achievements
Audit
IncompliancewiththeannouncementmadebytheMinisterforFinanceandHumanResourcesDevelopment in the 2021–2022 Budget Speech, the post of Director General of Audit was created to bring all audit departments under the control of the Finance Department, thereby ensuring coordinated oversight of audit functions.(Paragraph 4.4.1)
A Special Audit on field verification and genuineness of income certificates submitted for BC/MBC/MW/SC/ST scholarships and First Graduate certificates was conducted and the audit report was submitted to the Government.(Paragraph 4.4.2)
A Special Audit on the verification of Receipts and Expenditure relating to COVID-19 in five selected districts was conducted and the report was submitted to the Government.(Paragraph 4.4.3)
CapacityBuildingforAuditors
Training programmes on General Courses, IT-related courses, and domain-based courses are conducted periodically to improve audit quality.(Paragraph 4.4.4)
The e-Office system has been fully implemented (100%) in the State Government Audit Department to enable paperless transactions, improve file movement efficiency, and reduce delays in decision-making. (Paragraph 4.4.5)
Timeanalysisandcategorisationofauditobjectionsinallauditeeinstitutionshavebeencompleted.Thegist and number of pending audit paras, institution-wise, have been communicated to the concerned auditee institutions through e-mail, ensuring transparency.(Paragraph 4.4.6)
The system for approving statutory boards' audit reports has been revised — approval is now granted one level below, and issuance is carried out by the concerned Head of Office who conducted the audit, thereby avoiding delays.(Paragraph 4.4.7)
AsumofapproximatelyRs.28.41croreshasbeencollectedasauditfeesduringtheperiodfrom07.05.2021 to 31.09.2025.(Paragraph 4.4.8)
Government Order G.O.(Ms) No. 27, Finance (LF) Department, dated 04.02.2025, has been issued to constituteHighLevelCommittees(HLCs)—includingaStateLevelHighLevelCommitteeheadedbythe Finance Secretary and Departmental/Board Level High Level Committees — to facilitate the timely settlement of audit paras.(Paragraph 4.4.9)
The Director General of Audit has instructed all auditors to strictly follow the Code of Ethics during the course of audit.(Paragraph 4.4.10)
